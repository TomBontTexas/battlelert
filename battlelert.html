        How to play

        A career aboard the warships carrying humanity's planet buster. Take jobs as engineer or gunner (the pilot station is being built; try the helm test on the title screen) from the gunboat up to the dreadnought; a successful mission qualifies you on that class, and offers from bigger ships follow your reputation.

        The engine room

        The reactor can't feed everything at once, so you decide where the power goes, second by second.

        1. Read the forecast strip. Salvos, evasive burns and gun requests are announced seconds before they land.
        2. Shift charge to meet the next demand. Drag a capacitor's charge-rate slider, or select it with 1–9 and use ↑↓. The controller ramps toward your setting over a second or two, so start early.
        3. Keep heat under the line. Load over 100% heats the reactor. At full heat the failsafe trips capacitors off the reactor for 10 s, lowest priority first. Set the priority order so the right things trip.
        4. Spend aux power to bridge a gap. The aux bank charges a capacitor without loading the reactor. It only refills between jumps.
        5. Charge the GravSpace drive. When the jump window opens and the drive is full, the captain jumps. Three jumps and the planet buster is delivered.
        6. Every capacitor bleeds. The fuller it is the more it loses, so holding everything full keeps costing reactor power. Charge what the forecast says is coming.
        7. Bigger ships add more. Light cruisers and up have a main gun that fires by itself whenever its capacitor is full and a target is up. Cruisers and up carry gunboats: charge the launchers for the launch near the start of each system, then the arresters to recover them near the end. No jump until they're aboard, unless the hull is at 20% or less.
        8. The biggest ships. Heavy cruisers and up have a CIWS (close-in weapon system), the only thing that stops drive hunters' strikes on the GravSpace drive. Battleships have two reactors and dreadnoughts three: move capacitors between them to balance the load. The dreadnought also carries the planet buster, which must be full before the final jump.
        9. Minefields. No enemy fire, but the engines get only what the capacitors leave of the reactor. Ease off charging during each weave, or the ship drifts into mines.

        Shields stop salvos; whatever gets through damages the hull, and damage slows chargers, lowers reactor output and weakens the thrusters. Fed guns kill enemies faster, and a dead enemy fires no more salvos. Keep life support topped up, or the crew falters.

        Not every salvo is announced; surprises are rare. Pausing is free; looking at the console while paused costs 10% of the mission's score.

        At the gun

        Your ship sits in the middle of the view inside its oval shield, and enemies come in from every side, mostly ahead and on the flanks. Your engineer runs the power.

        1. Aim. Your gun rides the shield's rim inside its arc (the lit part of the rim) and fires straight out. It follows the mouse or trackpad around the ship; a finger on the view does the same, or drag on the aim pad; ← → turn it. Click or tap a ship or torpedo and the gun swings to it, holding fire until it's on.
        2. Fire. Hold Space, the mouse button or FIRE. The dotted line is your line of fire and the dashed curve your range. Rail and ion bolts stop at the range line; pulse bolts fly on past it but hit far weaker.
        3. Weapons. Each hits harder against its best targets: the pulse cannon (fast, light, short) against interceptors; the rail gun (slow, heavy, long) against raiders, siege lines and capital ships; the ion gun against drive hunters, and a hit drains the target's weapons for 4 s.
        4. Power. Every shot drains your gun's capacitor. Release FIRE to let it recharge, or call for power (C) and the engineer pours charge into it for a few seconds.
        5. Torpedoes. Enemy salvos are torpedoes; a ship about to fire glows red at the nose. The shields take them, but you can shoot one down first, and a dead ship fires no more.
        6. Several guns. You aim one; your gun crew works the others. They're people, not machines: each gunner has a skill (on the gun cards), reacts slower than you, aims rougher and now and then goes after the wrong target, so they need you. A fresh crew comes with every mission. Take a gun with its number key or its card; you trade places with its gunner. The letter on each card is its gunner's standing order: P point defense (torpedoes first), L stop launches (ships charging to fire first), H hunt (the ships its weapon is best against), F free fire. Take a gun and press O, or tap its letter, to change it; a green letter is the suggestion for this fight.
        7. Damage. Guns sit outside the shields. A torpedo that gets through can damage a gun's charger and turret: it turns slower, fires slower or loses range. Damage control repairs half of it between systems.
        8. Bigger ships add more. Gunboats carry two homing torpedoes (1 and 2), reloaded every sortie. Light cruisers and up have a main gun off the bow: press M when it's charged and the whole charge goes into the first ship on that line; the pilot swings the bow toward the biggest target, which tries to slide away. Cruisers launch a gunboat squadron that strafes the enemy and shoots down nearby torpedoes. In minefields, shoot the mines before they reach the shield.
        9. Drive hunters. From the heavy cruiser up, drive hunters fire teal missiles that swing around the shield into the stern, at the GravSpace drive. The CIWS mounts there fire on them by themselves while the CIWS has charge; one that gets through drains the drive. Shoot them down on the way in. The ion gun is best against the hunters.
        10. Archonian capital ships. One big hull with escorts, holding far out: a frigate (gunboats now and then), a cruiser (heavy cruisers and up) whose torpedoes come in pairs, and a battleship (battleships and dreadnoughts) whose torpedoes come in threes. Lighter ships keep joining the fight while it lasts. The pilot swings the main gun onto them, and rail guns do extra damage. On a dreadnought, the final system is the fortress guarding the Archonian world: it sits dead ahead, never moves and keeps firing until the planet buster is away, unless you destroy it first.
        11. Views and switches. W switches to wide view: the side panels give way to an overlay and the battle fills the screen, bow to the right. Switches on the left panel let you take a gun by clicking it on the view, or turn click-to-aim off.

        At the helm (test)

        The pilot flies the ship in the same view the gunner uses: your ship stays in the middle, bow up, and turning swings the battle around it. Your gun crew fires the guns and the engineer runs the power.

        1. Launch. A gunboat starts each sortie in the carrier's launch tube. Launch when ready (Space or LAUNCH); the catapult throws you out at flank, and once clear the coxswain orders all ahead standard: set both engines within 5 s (an order like any other; tap the order panel to set it). Waiting costs fight time, and after a while the carrier launches you anyway.
        2. Steer with the wheel. Drag it, or use ← →. It stays where you leave it, so the ship keeps turning until you center it (0). The ship answers a moment late, and its turn builds and dies away slowly; bigger ships are slower at both. The white tick on the compass and on the ring is where the bow will stop if you center the wheel now.
        3. Engine orders. Each engine takes its own order on the telegraph: stop, dead slow, cruise, standard, full, flank, or dead slow, half or full astern. + and − give an exact thrust ("all ahead 3 G"). Thrust draws reactor power, so a hard burn leaves less for the shields and guns; warn engineering (E) before one. Flank is over the engines' rating: hold it too long and a thruster can burn out.
        4. Split engines. Port ahead and starboard astern turns the ship to starboard faster than the wheel alone (and the other way round). Turning faster than the safe rate (the mark on the TURN bar) fills the hull stress bar; a full bar is a structural hit to the hull. Damage control reports the stress and the captain orders you to ease off, unless he ordered the hard turn himself (a break).
        5. The captain's orders. Only the captain (the coxswain on a gunboat) gives the helm orders; the crew report to the captain. He orders a heading and an engine order a few seconds ahead; the amber mark on the compass shows the heading. Be on it, at that order, when the time comes, and hold it. Between orders you're free to fight the ship: point the bow at a frigate and your gunner fires the torpedoes.
        6. Dodging is the captain's call. When torpedoes launch, Tactical reports them ("Vampire, vampire! Two new fish!") and the captain (the coxswain on a gunboat) decides: break ("Break left, now!", in red on the HELM panel) or take them on the shields ("Steady, Helm. Let the shields take it."). He takes them always while a course order is pending or under way, often when the shields can absorb them, and always on ships too big to swing in time. He keeps watching, and if the shields can no longer take the fish still in the air, he orders a break then. On a break, swing the bow 15° or more off your steady turn before the torpedoes land (the panel counts the degrees and the seconds) and they miss; a break made counts as an order met. The clock starts when you hear the order, not when the torpedo launches: a break covers its torpedoes for the full flight time after the order is spoken (turning as soon as it's on the panel counts too). You can still dodge on your own, but dodging a torpedo the captain said to take is disobeying an order: the captain calls you on it, it counts as an order not met, and each time costs 5% of the mission's score, and so your reputation. (Turning for the captain's own course order doesn't count, nor does steering in the few seconds after a break, such as straightening out.) Tactical's report cuts in on the voices, so the call comes at once. To keep orders clear, the helm doesn't hear the crew's running commentary (incoming-fire counts, contacts, kills, misses, "well done"): only the captain's orders, Tactical's torpedo reports, gunners' calls, launch control, jump calls, warnings and damage.
        7. Gunners call the captain (ships with guns that don't go all round). A gunner with nothing in their arc tells the captain ("Gunner 1: Captain, nothing in my arc. Target to port."), and he orders the helm round: a course order like any other. When the main gun is charged, the captain orders the bow onto the biggest ship. Gunners hold their calls while an order is pending.
        8. Jump heading (ships with a GravSpace drive). Navigation gives a jump heading on arrival (green on the compass), and as the window nears, the captain orders it as a course order (again if the bow wanders off). The ship jumps once the drive is full and the bow is on it (±10°); if the drive is full and you're still off it 20 s after the window opens, the captain takes the conn.
        9. Autopilot. T turns it on and holds the heading; tap the compass to fly to that heading, or drag the compass like a dial (it holds still and centers on the course you're setting) until the course you want is under the center pointer; or tap the captain's order to fly there. It flies carefully (never past the safe rate, never with split engines) and earns less reputation than steering by hand.
