Route

    Your ship

    Your station: Main Engineering

    • Each gun fires straight out from the shield's rim. A bolt hits the first ship or torpedo it reaches. Rail and ion bolts stop at the gun's range line (dashed); pulse bolts fly on past it but hit far weaker.
    • Enemy salvos come down as torpedoes. The shields take them, but you can shoot one down before it lands. Killing a ship stops the torpedoes it hasn't fired yet.
    • Your gun sits outside the shields. A torpedo that gets through can damage its charger and its turret: it turns slower, fires slower or loses range. Damage control repairs half of it between sorties.
    • Your gunboat carries two torpedoes: LAUNCH 1 and LAUNCH 2 (keys 1 and 2). They need no power and the carrier reloads them every sortie. Each one is slow and homes on the nearest frigate or siege battery; the target can dodge it or shoot it down, but a hit does more damage than anything else you carry.
    • Every shot drains your gun's capacitor. Hold FIRE and the gun fires as fast as it can while the charge lasts. Call for power and the engineer pours charge into your gun for a few seconds.
    • The main gun is yours. It points straight off the bow (the gold dashed line, bright when it's charged). Once the engineer has it full, press M or MAIN GUN: the whole charge goes into the first ship on that line, enough to gut most ships. With nothing on the line, the shot is wasted. The pilot swings the bow toward the biggest target, but it won't sit still and slides away from the bow, so be ready when it crosses.
    • In a minefield, mines drift in toward the ship. Shoot them before they reach the shield: each one that lands hits like a torpedo. When the engines run short of power the ship drifts, and more mines come.
    • Mouse or trackpad: point to turn the gun, click a target to swing to it, hold the button to fire. Touch: touch the view to point, or drag on the aim pad; hold FIRE. Keys: ← → turn, Space fires, C calls for power, P pauses.
    • The forecast strip at the top shows what the bridge needs in the next 15 seconds. Charge for it before it lands.
    • Every capacitor's charge rate adds to the reactor load. Above 100% the reactor heats up; at full heat the failsafe trips capacitors off the reactor for 10 s, lowest priority first.
    • The aux bank charges any capacitor you switch it onto, without loading the reactor. It refills only in GravSpace.
    • Capacitors that are full stop drawing power. When they're drained, they ramp back up to your setting over a second or two.
    • The main gun fires by itself the moment its capacitor is full and a target is up, spending the whole charge in one heavy shot. Keep feeding it and it keeps firing.
    • The hangar capacitor charges the electromagnetic launchers: they must be full when the bridge calls the launch, near the start of each system. While the gunboats are out they shoot down some salvos and strafe the enemy, and the same capacitor charges the arresters, which must be full to recover them near the end. The captain won't jump until the gunboats are aboard, unless the hull is at 20% or less.
    • Point defense is the only thing that stops a drive strike from drive hunters. Each strike it stops costs a chunk of its charge; one that gets through drains part of the GravSpace drive.
    • This ship has several reactors, each with its own load, heat, failsafe and aux bank. Every capacitor is wired to one reactor (the letter on its column); tap the letter or press R to move it to the next reactor. It draws nothing for 2 s while it switches. Aux banks can't be moved: each feeds only its own reactor's capacitors.
    • The planet buster charges from the start of the mission and keeps its charge, but it can't charge in GravSpace. It must be full, as well as the drive, before the final jump.
    • In a minefield there is no enemy fire, but the engines get only the power your capacitors leave. Ease off charging during each weave, or the ship drifts into mines.

        How to play

        You are the Destroyer's engineer. The reactor can't feed everything at once, so you decide where the power goes, second by second.

        1. Read the forecast strip. Salvos, evasive burns and gun requests are announced seconds before they land.
        2. Shift charge to meet the next demand. Drag a capacitor's charge-rate slider, or select it with 1–6 and use ↑↓. The controller ramps toward your setting over a second or two, so start early.
        3. Keep heat under the line. Load over 100% heats the reactor. At full heat the failsafe trips capacitors off the reactor for 10 s, lowest priority first. Set the priority order so the right things trip.
        4. Spend aux power to bridge a gap. The aux bank charges a capacitor without loading the reactor. It only refills between jumps.
        5. Charge the GravSpace drive. When the jump window opens and the drive is full, the captain jumps. Three jumps and the planet buster is delivered.
        6. Every capacitor bleeds. A charged capacitor slowly loses charge, and the fuller it is the more it loses, so holding everything full keeps costing reactor power. Charge what the forecast says is coming.
        7. Bigger ships add more. Light cruisers and up have a main gun that fires by itself whenever its capacitor is full and a target is up; keep feeding it and it keeps firing. Cruisers and up carry gunboats: charge the launchers for the launch near the start of each system, then the arresters to recover them near the end. No jump until they're aboard, unless the hull is at 20% or less.
        8. The biggest ships. Heavy cruisers and up have point defense, the only thing that stops drive hunters' strikes on the GravSpace drive. Battleships have two reactors and dreadnoughts three: move capacitors between them to balance the load. The dreadnought also carries the planet buster, which must be full before the final jump.
        9. Minefields. No enemy fire, but the engines get only what the capacitors leave of the reactor. Ease off charging during each weave, or the ship drifts into mines.

        Shields stop salvos; whatever gets through damages the hull, and damage slows chargers, lowers reactor output and weakens the thrusters. Fed guns kill enemies faster, and a dead enemy fires no more salvos. Keep life support topped up, or the crew falters.

        Not every salvo is announced; surprises are rare. Pausing is free; looking at the console while paused costs 10% of the mission's score.
