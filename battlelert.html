Route

    Your ship

    Your station: Main Engineering

    • The helm: each sortie starts in the carrier's launch tube; launch when ready (Space). The coxswain gives course and engine orders ahead of time. Your gunner fires the gun, and both torpedoes once a frigate or siege battery is within 20° of the bow. His course stands between orders, and you may swing up to 30° either side of it to dodge torpedoes (outside that corridor is disobeying and costs you). When the shields can't take what's coming he orders a break ("Break left, now!": swing the bow 15° off your steady turn before they land and they miss). Only the coxswain gives you orders; the crew just report. When you clear the carrier, set all ahead standard: that's an order.

        How to play

        A career aboard the warships carrying humanity's planet buster. Take jobs as engineer, gunner or pilot from the gunboat up to the dreadnought. Each station has its own storyline of the war: three story missions per ship class, in any order, each offered until you complete it, and the dreadnought's three are versions of the storyline's finale (winning one completes it). Story missions you skip show under Requalifying jobs at half reputation; a successful mission qualifies you on that class, and offers from bigger ships follow your reputation. Every ship is named after a real warship and keeps a service record in your career file. A ship destroyed or abandoned is lost: its record is closed, and the next ship of that name is a new one with a new hull number (a lost gunboat's PT number never comes back). The career hub's Naval registry lists every ship by class and tech level; tap one for its card: its picture, its statistics and its service career.

        The engine room

        The reactor can't feed everything at once, so you decide where the power goes, second by second.

        1. Read the forecast strip. Salvos, evasive burns and gun requests are announced seconds before they land.
        2. Shift charge to meet the next demand. Drag a capacitor's charge-rate slider, or select it with 1–9 and use ↑↓. The controller ramps toward your setting over a second or two, so start early.
        3. Keep heat under the line. Load over 100% heats the reactor. At full heat the failsafe trips capacitors off the reactor for 10 s, lowest priority first. Set the priority order so the right things trip.
        4. Spend aux power to bridge a gap. The aux bank charges a capacitor without loading the reactor. It only refills between jumps.
        5. Charge the warp drive. When the jump window opens and the drive is full, the captain jumps. Three jumps and the planet buster is delivered.
        6. Every capacitor bleeds. The fuller it is the more it loses, so holding everything full keeps costing reactor power. Charge what the forecast says is coming.
        7. Bigger ships add more. Destroyer escorts and up have a CIWS (close-in weapon system): it stops most drive hunters' strikes on the warp drive while it has charge (each costs a chunk of it), though now and then one gets through. Light cruisers and up have a main gun that fires by itself whenever its capacitor is full and a target is up. Cruisers and up carry gunboats: charge the launchers for the launch near the start of each system, then the arresters to recover them near the end. No jump until they're aboard, unless the hull is at 20% or less.
        8. The biggest ships. Battleships have two reactors and dreadnoughts three: move capacitors between them to balance the load. The shields draw evenly from every reactor (A+B), like the engines, so turning them up spreads across them all; switched to aux, they take an even share from every reactor's aux bank too. Every other capacitor's aux comes from its own reactor's bank. The dreadnought also carries the planet buster, which must be full before the final jump.
        9. Minefields. No enemy fire, but the engines get only what the capacitors leave of the reactor. Ease off charging during each weave, or the ship drifts into mines.

        Shields stop salvos; whatever gets through damages the hull, and damage slows chargers, lowers reactor output and weakens the thrusters. Fed guns kill enemies faster, and a dead enemy fires no more salvos. Keep life support topped up, or the crew falters.

        Not every salvo is announced; surprises are rare. Pausing is free; looking at the console while paused costs 10% of the mission's score.

        For testing, every station has a Test win button in its top bar: it ends the mission at once as a success scored 75%, earning that reputation and qualifying you on the class.

        The enemy

        Azurian ships fire torpedoes in three sizes: darts (small and fast, hard to shoot down, light hits: interceptors), torpedoes (raiders, frigates, cruisers) and ship killers (slow and easy to hit, but huge: cruisers now and then, battleships and the fortress). They also carry guns: once a wave closes ("Tactical: they're in gun range."), its ships keep up a steady fire that only the shields stop. Drive hunters (destroyer escort and up) fire missiles at the warp drive, and mines are the same size everywhere. An enemy is equally strong whatever ship you're on and whatever station you serve at; bigger ships meet more of them, and bigger ones. When several groups are due at once, they come in a few seconds apart, one after another. A group that has lost ships fires fewer torpedoes, at every station: in the engine room, where you can't see its ships, the more of it the guns have destroyed, the fewer of its salvos come. Our guns carry a Mk number, the ship's class (Mk1 on a gunboat to Mk9 on a dreadnought): each Mk hits harder.

        At the gun

        Your ship sits in the middle of the view inside its oval shield, and enemies come in from every side, mostly ahead and on the flanks. Your engineer runs the power.

        1. Aim. Your gun rides the shield's rim inside its arc (the lit part of the rim) and fires straight out. It follows the mouse or trackpad around the ship; a finger on the view does the same, or drag on the aim pad; ← → turn it. Click or tap a ship or torpedo and the gun swings to it, holding fire until it's on.
        2. Fire. Hold Space, the mouse button or FIRE. The dotted line is your line of fire and the dashed curve your range. Rail and ion bolts stop at the range line; pulse bolts fly on past it but hit far weaker.
        3. Weapons. Each hits harder against its best targets: the pulse cannon (fast, light, short) against interceptors; the rail gun (slow, heavy, long) against raiders, siege lines and capital ships; the ion gun against drive hunters, and a hit drains the target's weapons for 4 s.
        4. Power. Every shot drains your gun's capacitor. Release FIRE to let it recharge, or call for power (C) and the engineer pours charge into it for a few seconds.
        5. Torpedoes. Enemy salvos are torpedoes; a ship about to fire glows red at the nose. The shields take them, but you can shoot one down first, and a dead ship fires no more.
        6. Several guns. You aim one; your gun crew works the others. They're people, not machines: each gunner has a skill (on the gun cards), reacts slower than you, aims rougher and now and then goes after the wrong target, so they need you. A fresh crew comes with every mission. Take a gun with its number key or its card; you trade places with its gunner. The letter on each card is its gunner's standing order: P point defense (torpedoes first), L stop launches (ships charging to fire first), H hunt (the ships its weapon is best against), F free fire. Take a gun and press O, or tap its letter, to change it; a green letter is the suggestion for this fight.
        7. Damage. Guns sit outside the shields. A torpedo that gets through can damage a gun's charger and turret: it turns slower, fires slower or loses range. Damage control repairs half of it between systems.
        8. Bigger ships add more. Gunboats carry two homing torpedoes (1 and 2), reloaded every sortie. Light cruisers and up have a main gun off the bow: press M when it's charged and the whole charge goes into the first ship on that line; the pilot swings the bow toward the biggest target, which tries to slide away. Cruisers launch a gunboat squadron that strafes the enemy and shoots down nearby torpedoes. In minefields the mines sit still and stream past from ahead as the ship moves through them (nobody steers round them at the gun): shoot the ones on a collision course before they reach the shield.
        9. Drive hunters. From the destroyer escort up, drive hunters fire teal missiles that swing around the shield into the stern, at the warp drive. The CIWS mounts there fire on them by themselves while the CIWS has charge and stop most of them; one that gets through drains the drive. Shoot them down on the way in. The ion gun is best against the hunters.
        10. Azurian capital ships. One big hull with escorts, holding far out: a frigate (gunboats now and then), a cruiser (heavy cruisers and up) whose torpedoes come in pairs, and a battleship (battleships and dreadnoughts) whose torpedoes come in threes. Lighter ships keep joining the fight while it lasts. The pilot swings the main gun onto them, and rail guns do extra damage. On a dreadnought, the final system is the fortress guarding the Azurian world: it sits dead ahead, never moves and keeps firing until the planet buster is away, unless you destroy it first.
        11. Views and switches. W switches to wide view: the side panels give way to an overlay and the battle fills the screen, bow to the right. Switches on the left panel let you take a gun by clicking it on the view, or turn click-to-aim off.

        At the helm

        The pilot flies the ship in the same view the gunner uses: your ship stays in the middle, bow up, and turning swings the battle around it. Your gun crew fires the guns and the engineer runs the power. Pilot jobs (career hub) run from the gunboat to the dreadnought, and each names the engineer and the gun crew; the helm test on the title screen tries any ship without touching your career. Your pilot profile tracks aggression (hard turns and flank), following orders, dodging (the breaks you're ordered and make), guns on target (enemy ships your crew destroys) and ship condition.

        1. Launch. A gunboat starts each sortie in the carrier's launch tube. Launch when ready (Space or LAUNCH); the catapult throws you out at flank, and once clear the coxswain orders all ahead standard: set both engines within 5 s (an order like any other; tap the order panel to set it). Waiting costs fight time, and after a while the carrier launches you anyway.
        2. Steer with the wheel. Drag it, or use ← →. It stays where you leave it, so the ship keeps turning until you center it (0). The ship answers a moment late, and its turn builds and dies away slowly; bigger ships are slower at both. The white tick on the compass and on the ring is where the bow will stop if you center the wheel now.
        3. Engine orders. Each engine takes its own order on the telegraph: stop, dead slow, cruise, standard, full, flank, or dead slow, half or full astern. + and − give an exact thrust ("all ahead 3 G"). Thrust draws reactor power, so a hard burn leaves less for the shields and guns; warn engineering (E) before one. Flank is over the engines' rating: hold it too long and a thruster can burn out.
        4. Engine damage. A thruster hit damages one engine, port or starboard: it gives less thrust at every speed (each hit about 15%), so the stronger engine pushes the bow toward the damaged side and the ship drifts. The panel shows it ("⚠ Engine damage: Port 85%", with what each engine is giving). Steer against it, or trim the engines with the P −/+ and S −/+ buttons (W S, O K): a quarter G at a time, ease the strong engine back or push the weak one up until both give the same thrust and the drift stops. The autopilot steers against the drift for you and still keeps her under the safe rate; by hand, a turn with the drift goes faster than the wheel alone and can overstress the hull. In warp space damage control repairs half of it.
        5. Split engines. Port ahead and starboard astern turns the ship to starboard faster than the wheel alone (and the other way round). Turning faster than the safe rate (the mark on the TURN bar) fills the hull stress bar; a full bar is a structural hit to the hull. Damage control reports the stress and the captain orders you to ease off, unless he ordered the hard turn himself (a break).
        6. The captain's orders. Only the captain (the coxswain on a gunboat) gives the helm orders; the crew report to the captain. He orders a heading and an engine order a few seconds ahead. The order panel shows it at a glance (when, heading, engines: "In 5s / Left 255 / All ahead Standard"; tap it to fly the heading on autopilot), and the amber mark on the compass shows the heading. Be on it, at that order, when the time comes, and hold it: you have 2 s of grace after NOW, then must be on both the heading (±10°) and the engine order for 70% of the rest. The panel ticks each part (✔ right, ✗ wrong while the order is under way), and if you miss, the captain says which part. Orders he gives on the spot (a gunner's call, the main gun, the jump heading) come with at least 6 s notice. Between orders you're free to fight the ship: point the bow at a frigate and your gunner fires the torpedoes.
        7. Minefields (bigger ships). A steering test: the captain gives no course orders there. The mines sit still and the ship moves through them: they stream past from ahead as you go (from astern going astern), faster and more often the harder you burn, and not at all while you're stopped. Turning swings the whole field around you. Steer between them: one that touches the shield hits hard, and holding course through the field will usually cost you the ship. The mines are small, so your gun crew only picks off some.
        8. Mine lane (gunboat tests, sortie 2). Sweep a lane through a minefield so the fleet behind can get through: no enemy ships, just mines streaming past as you go. Your gunner shoots them; fly so they can (slower gives the gun more time per mine) and don't run into any. The report shows the share of the lane cleared. At the gun, the coxswain holds the boat at standard and you shoot.
        9. The corridor. The captain's course stands until his next order: the course he gave last (on arrival, the way the ship is heading). You may swing up to 30° either side of it, so dodging inside that corridor is yours to do: a torpedo misses if the bow ends 15° or more off where the enemy aimed when it fired. The corridor is the green band on the compass and the two marks on the ring; it turns red once you're outside it, and the captain says so ("Helm, you're outside the corridor."). Out of the corridor between course orders is disobeying: it counts as an order not met and costs 5% of the mission's score each time, and so your reputation. During a course order the order is judged on its own: the bow inside the corridor and the engines at the ordered speed. There's no corridor while you turn onto a new order, during a break, in a minefield, or once the captain has the conn. Lined up for the jump, from 10 s before the window and once the warp drive is 95% charged (no jump until it's full), the captain says "Steady as she goes." and the corridor narrows to 5° either side of the jump heading. The ship jumps only with the bow inside that corridor: off it, the jump (even its countdown) waits until you're back. The order panel shows the course that stands: COURSE with its heading and ±30°, STEADY on the jump heading, and for a few seconds after an order ends, how it went ("Order met ✔", "Missed: heading ✗").
        10. Breaks. When the shields can't take the torpedoes on their way (and now and then when they can), the captain (the coxswain on a gunboat) orders a break ("Break left, now!", in red on the HELM panel); never two within 6 s, and not on ships too big to swing in time. He keeps watching the torpedoes he lets come, and if the shields can no longer take them, he orders a break then. The clock starts when you hear the break order, not at launch: its torpedoes take their full flight time after it. Swing the bow 15° or more off your steady turn before they land (the panel counts the degrees and the seconds) and they miss; a break made counts as an order met. A break order cuts in on the voices, so you hear it at once. To keep orders clear, the helm doesn't hear the crew's running commentary (incoming-fire counts, contacts, kills, misses, "well done"): only the captain's orders, gunners' calls, launch control, jump calls, warnings and damage.
        11. Gunners call the captain (ships with guns that don't go all round). A gunner with nothing in his arc tells the captain ("Gunner 1: Captain, nothing in my arc. Target to port."), and he orders the helm round: a course order like any other. When the main gun is charged, the captain orders the bow onto the biggest ship. Gunners hold their calls while an order is pending.
        12. Jump heading (ships with a warp drive). Navigation gives a jump heading on arrival (green on the compass), and as the window nears, the captain orders it as a course order (again if the bow wanders off). The ship jumps once the drive is full and the bow is on it (±10°); if the drive is full and you're still off it 20 s after the window opens, the captain takes the conn.
        13. Autopilot. T turns it on and holds the heading; tap the compass across the top to fly to that heading, or tap the captain's order to fly there. On autopilot the strip at the far right of the screen (clear of the battle) is the course set: drag it up and down (headings rise upward, blue box in the middle; the strip's whole height is 360°, so one sweep of the thumb reaches any heading) or tap a heading on it, and the autopilot speeds up and slows the turn to bring her onto it. For the last degree or two, the thin ▲ +1° and ▼ −1° buttons at the ends of the strip (or ] and [) nudge the course one degree; by hand they put her on autopilot one degree off the heading she's on. The compass across the top always shows the ship's actual heading. By hand (autopilot off) the strip is a wheel that slides up and down instead, with no numbers: the farther you slide it from the middle, the harder she turns (down for starboard, up for port), and it stays where you leave it; bringing her onto the heading is up to you. It flies carefully (never past the safe rate, even with a damaged engine; never with split engines) and earns less reputation than steering by hand.

        On the bridge

        The captain plots the course and gives the orders; his crew carry them out. For now the bridge is a test on the title screen (Captain bridge test, a destroyer), with no career effect.

        1. Plot the course. In warp space before each system, with the clock stopped, the plotting screen shows the system's map: where you come out of warp space, the jump zone (green), asteroid fields (grey) and enemy areas (red), in all shapes, with one always across the straight way to the jump. Each system's jump heading is set for you: the green arrow through the zone, with a dashed approach line leading in. Tap the map to lay waypoints: 1 to 8 legs to the jump zone, the last one along the jump heading (within 10°; Accept stays off until it is). A new plot starts on the approach line, and waypoints you lay go in before it. Tap a waypoint to remove it, and a leg's engine order (cruise, standard, full) to change it. The time line shows when the course arrives against the jump window. A leg through an asteroid field or an enemy area shows red, and Accept stays off until the course goes round them all. Navigator's plot fills in a safe course to accept or edit. The last leg's heading is the jump heading.
        2. The pilot flies it. He steers for the plotted legs and gives the engines each leg's order. NAVIGATION on the BRIDGE panel shows the leg, the next course change and the jump window; the map at the right shows the ship on the plot. In the jump zone he holds her there, dead slow and then stopped, for the jump.
        3. The map. Its top line counts down to the jump zone at her speed now, along the rest of the plot: faster and it drops, slower and it climbs. Against the window it says EARLY (slow down, or she waits in the zone), ON TIME or LATE (more speed). Drag it to pan, pinch or use + − to zoom, FIT for the whole map. ⇄ SWAP (V) puts the map in the middle and the battle view in its box; swap again to go back.
        4. A course of your own. ← → (or ◀ 10° and 10° ▶), a tap on the compass, or a tap on the map gives the helm a course of your own, and the engine buttons an engine order. HELM shows "yours" until you tap RESUME PLOT (R); then the pilot comes back onto the plotted line, never cutting across a field.
        5. Torpedoes. On every burst at you, the call is yours: BREAK PORT, TAKE IT or BREAK STBD (Z X C) before they land. A break the pilot makes in time makes them miss. Your standing order is to take them on the shields, so a burst you don't answer is taken. Break away from asteroid fields: the map shows where they are.
        6. The crew. The gunners' order (G) goes to every gun: SUGGESTED (each fight's suggestion), TORPEDOES (point defense: torpedoes about to land first), LAUNCHERS (ships charging to fire first), HUNT (the ships each gun is best against) or FREE FIRE (each gunner picks his own). The engineer's stance (S) is his priority order: shields first, balanced, or guns first. Warn engineering (E) before a hard burn.
        7. Asteroids. A ship inside an asteroid field takes a hit like a mine (90 MJ, shields first) every few seconds. A plotted leg that's clear keeps her out of them.
        8. The jump. Order it (J or JUMP) once the drive is full, the window is open, the ship is in the zone and she's on the jump heading (within 5°); the JUMP button says what's missing ("Steer 245"). Keeping her on the jump heading is your job: on the plot the pilot flies the last leg along it, and he holds her in the zone; on a course of your own, steer it yourself or RESUME PLOT. An ordered jump waits until she's in the zone (BACK TO THE ZONE) and on the heading (STEER 245), then goes.
        9. Score. Objective and ship condition as everywhere, navigation (in the jump zone by the window, no asteroid hits) and command (the shields and guns against the torpedoes, and the breaks you ordered made).

        Pilot training

        From the career screen, Pilot training puts you at the helm of any ship you're qualified on as a pilot (the gunboat if you aren't yet) on an endless buoy course: no enemies, no captain's orders, all the usual controls. Keep red buoys to port (on your left) and green to starboard; the next one is ringed, with a chevron on the side to pass, and marked in its color on the compass and the ring. The helm panel shows its side, bearing and seconds away, and a tally: passed on the right side, wrong side, hit (the hull touched it) and missed (too wide, or you wandered off and the course starts again ahead). The rating is the share passed on the right side: Excellent 90% and up, Good 75%, Fair 50%, else Needs work. Turning too hard still fills the stress bar and counts structural hits, but does the hull no harm. Fly as long as you like; Exit training shows the summary. Training earns no reputation and isn't saved to your record.
