Route

    Your ship

    Your station: Main Engineering

    • The helm: each sortie starts in the carrier's launch tube; launch when ready (Space). The coxswain gives course and engine orders ahead of time. Your gunner fires the gun, and both torpedoes once a frigate or siege battery is within 20° of the bow. His course stands between orders, and you may swing up to 30° either side of it to dodge torpedoes (outside that corridor is disobeying and costs you). When the shields can't take what's coming he orders a break ("Break left, now!": swing the bow 15° off your steady turn before they land and they miss). Only the coxswain gives you orders; the crew just report. When you clear the carrier, set all ahead standard: that's an order.
    • The bridge: in warp space before each system you plot its course to the warp zone, or take the navigator's plot. Your pilot flies it; the crew carry out your orders. Standing orders: the gunners FREE, the engineer BALANCE, the pilot HOLD. Each man takes a few seconds to carry out a new order, longer the lower his skill.
    • Two torpedoes (LAUNCH 1 and 2, keys 1 and 2): slow, they home on a frigate or siege battery and hit harder than anything else you carry. Reloaded every sortie.
    • Main gun: press M when it's charged; it hits the first ship straight off the bow (the gold line). The pilot swings the bow toward the biggest target, which slides away, so time your shot.
    • Gunboat squadron: launched early in each system, it strafes the enemy and shoots down nearby torpedoes, then comes home before the warp.
    • CIWS: its mounts in the stern fire on drive hunters' missiles by themselves while the engineer keeps it charged, and stop most of them; now and then one gets through. Your guns can shoot them down too.
    • Minefield: the mines sit still and stream past from ahead as the ship moves; shoot the ones on a collision course before they reach the shield.
    • Mine lane (sortie 2): clear a lane through a minefield so the fleet behind can get through. Shoot as many mines as you can; one that touches your shield hits. The report shows the share of the lane cleared.
    • Minefield: the mines sit still and stream past as you move; steer between them. More thrust brings them faster and more often.

        How to play

        A career aboard the warships carrying humanity's planet buster. Take jobs as engineer, gunner or pilot from the gunboat up to the dreadnought. Each station has its own storyline of the war: three story missions per ship class, in any order, each offered until you complete it, and the dreadnought's three are versions of the storyline's finale (winning one completes it). Story missions you skip show under Requalifying jobs at half reputation; a successful mission qualifies you on that class, and offers from bigger ships follow your reputation. Every ship is named after a real warship and keeps a service record in your career file. A ship destroyed or abandoned is lost: its record is closed, and the next ship of that name is a new one with a new hull number (a lost gunboat's PT number never comes back). The career hub's Naval registry lists every ship by class and tech level; tap one for its card: its picture, its statistics and its service career.

        The engine room

        The reactor can't feed everything at once, so you decide where the power goes, second by second.

        1. Read the forecast strip. Salvos, evasive burns and gun requests are announced seconds before they land.
        2. Shift charge to meet the next demand. Drag a capacitor's charge-rate slider, or select it with 1–9 and use ↑↓. The controller ramps toward your setting over a second or two, so start early.
        3. Keep heat under the line. Load over 100% heats the reactor. At full heat the failsafe trips capacitors off the reactor for 10 s, lowest priority first. Set the priority order so the right things trip.
        4. Spend aux power to bridge a gap. The aux bank charges a capacitor without loading the reactor. It only refills between warps.
        5. Charge the warp drive. When the warp window opens and the drive is full, the captain warps. On a dreadnought the last system is the drop: no warp out; once the planet buster is full and the window opens, it's launched at the Azurian target world (industrial, military or transportation). Miss the drop window (10 s after it opens) and the mission fails.
        6. Every capacitor bleeds. The fuller it is the more it loses, so holding everything full keeps costing reactor power. Charge what the forecast says is coming.
        7. Bigger ships add more. Destroyer escorts and up have a CIWS (close-in weapon system): it stops most drive hunters' strikes on the warp drive while it has charge (each costs a chunk of it), though now and then one gets through. Light cruisers and up have a main gun that fires by itself whenever its capacitor is full and a target is up. Cruisers and up carry gunboats: charge the launchers for the launch near the start of each system, then the arresters to recover them near the end. No warp until they're aboard, unless the hull is at 20% or less.
        8. The biggest ships. Battleships have two reactors and dreadnoughts three: move capacitors between them to balance the load. The shields draw evenly from every reactor (A+B), like the engines, so turning them up spreads across them all; switched to aux, they take an even share from every reactor's aux bank too. Every other capacitor's aux comes from its own reactor's bank. The dreadnought also carries the planet buster, which must be full before the final warp.
        9. Minefields. No enemy fire, but the engines get only what the capacitors leave of the reactor. Ease off charging during each weave, or the ship drifts into mines.

        Shields stop salvos; whatever gets through damages the hull, and damage slows chargers, lowers reactor output and weakens the thrusters. Fed guns kill enemies faster, and a dead enemy fires no more salvos. Keep life support topped up, or the crew falters.

        Not every salvo is announced; surprises are rare. Pausing is free; looking at the console while paused costs 10% of the mission's score.

        For testing, every station has a Test win button in its top bar: it ends the mission at once as a success scored 75%, earning that reputation and qualifying you on the class.

        The enemy

        Azurian ships fire torpedoes in three sizes: darts (small and fast, hard to shoot down, light hits: interceptors), torpedoes (raiders, frigates, cruisers) and ship killers (slow and easy to hit, but huge: cruisers now and then, battleships and the fortress). They also carry guns: once a wave closes ("Tactical: they're in gun range."), its ships keep up a steady fire that only the shields stop. Drive hunters (destroyer escort and up) fire missiles at the warp drive, and mines are the same size everywhere. An enemy is equally strong whatever ship you're on and whatever station you serve at; bigger ships meet more of them, and bigger ones. When several groups are due at once, they come in a few seconds apart, one after another. A group that has lost ships fires fewer torpedoes, at every station: in the engine room, where you can't see its ships, the more of it the guns have destroyed, the fewer of its salvos come. Our guns carry a Mk number, the ship's class (Mk1 on a gunboat to Mk9 on a dreadnought): each Mk hits harder.

        At the gun

        Your ship sits in the middle of the view inside its oval shield, and enemies come in from every side, mostly ahead and on the flanks. Your engineer runs the power.

        1. Aim. Your gun rides the shield's rim inside its arc (the lit part of the rim) and fires straight out. It follows the mouse or trackpad around the ship; a finger on the view does the same, or drag on the aim pad; ← → turn it. Click or tap a ship or torpedo and the gun swings to it, holding fire until it's on.
        2. Fire. Hold Space, the mouse button or FIRE. The dotted line is your line of fire and the dashed curve your range. Rail and ion bolts stop at the range line; pulse bolts fly on past it but hit far weaker.
        3. Weapons. Each hits harder against its best targets: the pulse cannon (fast, light, short) against interceptors; the rail gun (slow, heavy, long) against raiders, siege lines and capital ships; the ion gun against drive hunters, and a hit drains the target's weapons for 4 s.
        4. Power. Every shot drains your gun's capacitor. Release FIRE to let it recharge, or call for power (C) and the engineer pours charge into it for a few seconds.
        5. Torpedoes. Enemy salvos are torpedoes; a ship about to fire glows red at the nose. The shields take them, but you can shoot one down first, and a dead ship fires no more.
        6. Several guns. You aim one; your gun crew works the others. They're people, not machines: each gunner has a skill (on the gun cards), reacts slower than you, aims rougher and now and then goes after the wrong target, so they need you. A fresh crew comes with every mission. Take a gun with its number key or its card; you trade places with its gunner. The letter on each card is its gunner's standing order: P point defense (torpedoes first), L stop launches (ships charging to fire first), H hunt (the ships its weapon is best against), F free fire. Take a gun and press O, or tap its letter, to change it; a green letter is the suggestion for this fight.
        7. Damage. Guns sit outside the shields. A torpedo that gets through can damage a gun's charger and turret: it turns slower, fires slower or loses range. Damage control repairs half of it between systems.
        8. Bigger ships add more. Gunboats carry two homing torpedoes (1 and 2), reloaded every sortie. Light cruisers and up have a main gun off the bow: press M when it's charged and the whole charge goes into the first ship on that line; the pilot swings the bow toward the biggest target, which tries to slide away. Cruisers launch a gunboat squadron that strafes the enemy and shoots down nearby torpedoes. In minefields the mines sit still and stream past from ahead as the ship moves through them (nobody steers round them at the gun): shoot the ones on a collision course before they reach the shield.
        9. Drive hunters. From the destroyer escort up, drive hunters fire teal missiles that swing around the shield into the stern, at the warp drive. The CIWS mounts there fire on them by themselves while the CIWS has charge and stop most of them; one that gets through drains the drive. Shoot them down on the way in. The ion gun is best against the hunters.
        10. Azurian capital ships. One big hull with escorts, holding far out: a frigate (gunboats now and then), a cruiser (heavy cruisers and up) whose torpedoes come in pairs, and a battleship (battleships and dreadnoughts) whose torpedoes come in threes. Lighter ships keep joining the fight while it lasts. The pilot swings the main gun onto them, and rail guns do extra damage. On a dreadnought, the final system is the fortress guarding the Azurian world: it sits dead ahead, never moves and keeps firing until the planet buster is away, unless you destroy it first.
        11. Views and switches. W switches to wide view: the side panels give way to an overlay and the battle fills the screen, bow to the right. Switches on the left panel let you take a gun by clicking it on the view, or turn click-to-aim off.

        At the helm

        The pilot flies the ship in the same view the gunner uses: your ship stays in the middle, bow up, and turning swings the battle around it. Your gun crew fires the guns and the engineer runs the power. Pilot jobs (career hub) run from the gunboat to the dreadnought, and each names the engineer and the gun crew; the Pilot helm test (title screen, Tests) tries any ship without touching your career. Your pilot profile tracks aggression (hard turns and flank), following orders, dodging (the breaks you're ordered and make), guns on target (enemy ships your crew destroys) and ship condition.

        1. Launch. A gunboat starts each sortie in the carrier's launch tube. Launch when ready (Space or LAUNCH); the catapult throws you out at flank, and once clear the coxswain orders all ahead standard: set both engines within 5 s (an order like any other; tap the order panel to set it). Waiting costs fight time, and after a while the carrier launches you anyway.
        2. Steer with the wheel. Drag it, or use ← →. It stays where you leave it, so the ship keeps turning until you center it (0). The ship answers a moment late, and its turn builds and dies away slowly; bigger ships are slower at both. The white tick on the compass and on the ring is where the bow will stop if you center the wheel now.
        3. Engine orders. Each engine takes its own order on the telegraph: stop, slow, cruise, standard, full, flank, or slow, half or full astern (spoken orders still say "dead slow"). + and − give an exact thrust ("all ahead 3 G"). Thrust draws reactor power, so a hard burn leaves less for the shields and guns; warn engineering (E) before one. Flank is over the engines' rating: hold it too long and a thruster can burn out.
        4. Engine damage. A thruster hit damages one engine, port or starboard: it gives less thrust at every speed (each hit about 15%), so the stronger engine pushes the bow toward the damaged side and the ship drifts. The panel shows it ("⚠ Engine damage: Port 85%", with what each engine is giving). Steer against it, or trim the engines with the P −/+ and S −/+ buttons (W S, O K): a quarter G at a time, ease the strong engine back or push the weak one up until both give the same thrust and the drift stops. The autopilot steers against the drift for you and still keeps her under the safe rate; by hand, a turn with the drift goes faster than the wheel alone and can overstress the hull. In warp space damage control repairs half of it.
        5. Split engines. Port ahead and starboard astern turns the ship to starboard faster than the wheel alone (and the other way round). Turning faster than the safe rate (the mark on the TURN bar) fills the hull stress bar; a full bar is a structural hit to the hull. Damage control reports the stress and the captain orders you to ease off, unless he ordered the hard turn himself (a break).
        6. The captain's orders. Only the captain (the coxswain on a gunboat) gives the helm orders; the crew report to the captain. He orders a heading and an engine order a few seconds ahead. The order panel shows it at a glance (when, heading, engines: "In 5s / Left 255 / All ahead Standard"; tap it to fly the heading on autopilot), and the amber mark on the compass shows the heading. Be on it, at that order, when the time comes, and hold it: you have 2 s of grace after NOW, then must be on both the heading (±10°) and the engine order for 70% of the rest. The panel ticks each part (✔ right, ✗ wrong while the order is under way), and if you miss, the captain says which part. Orders he gives on the spot (a gunner's call, the main gun, the warp heading) come with at least 6 s notice. Between orders you're free to fight the ship: point the bow at a frigate and your gunner fires the torpedoes.
        7. Minefields (bigger ships). A steering test: the captain gives no course orders there. The mines sit still and the ship moves through them: they stream past from ahead as you go (from astern going astern), faster and more often the harder you burn, and not at all while you're stopped. Turning swings the whole field around you. Steer between them: one that touches the shield hits hard, and holding course through the field will usually cost you the ship. The mines are small, so your gun crew only picks off some.
        8. Mine lane (gunboat tests, sortie 2). Sweep a lane through a minefield so the fleet behind can get through: no enemy ships, just mines streaming past as you go. Your gunner shoots them; fly so they can (slower gives the gun more time per mine) and don't run into any. The report shows the share of the lane cleared. At the gun, the coxswain holds the boat at standard and you shoot.
        9. The corridor. The captain's course stands until his next order: the course he gave last (on arrival, the way the ship is heading). You may swing up to 30° either side of it, so dodging inside that corridor is yours to do: a torpedo misses if the bow ends 15° or more off where the enemy aimed when it fired. The corridor is the green band on the compass and the two marks on the ring; it turns red once you're outside it, and the captain says so ("Helm, you're outside the corridor."). Out of the corridor between course orders is disobeying: it counts as an order not met and costs 5% of the mission's score each time, and so your reputation. During a course order the order is judged on its own: the bow inside the corridor and the engines at the ordered speed. There's no corridor while you turn onto a new order, during a break, in a minefield, or once the captain has the conn. Lined up for the warp, from 10 s before the window and once the warp drive is 95% charged (no warp until it's full), the captain says "Steady as she goes." and the corridor narrows to 5° either side of the warp heading. The ship warps only with the bow inside that corridor: off it, the warp (even its countdown) waits until you're back. The order panel shows the course that stands: COURSE with its heading and ±30°, STEADY on the warp heading, and for a few seconds after an order ends, how it went ("Order met ✔", "Missed: heading ✗").
        10. Breaks. When the shields can't take the torpedoes on their way (and now and then when they can), the captain (the coxswain on a gunboat) orders a break ("Break left, now!", in red on the HELM panel); never two within 6 s, and not on ships too big to swing in time. He keeps watching the torpedoes he lets come, and if the shields can no longer take them, he orders a break then. The clock starts when you hear the break order, not at launch: its torpedoes take their full flight time after it. Swing the bow 15° or more off your steady turn before they land (the panel counts the degrees and the seconds) and they miss; a break made counts as an order met. A break order cuts in on the voices, so you hear it at once. To keep orders clear, the helm doesn't hear the crew's running commentary (incoming-fire counts, contacts, kills, misses, "well done"): only the captain's orders, gunners' calls, launch control, warp calls, warnings and damage.
        11. Gunners call the captain (ships with guns that don't go all round). A gunner with nothing in his arc tells the captain ("Gunner 1: Captain, nothing in my arc. Target to port."), and he orders the helm round: a course order like any other. When the main gun is charged, the captain orders the bow onto the biggest ship. Gunners hold their calls while an order is pending.
        12. Warp heading (ships with a warp drive). Navigation gives a warp heading on arrival (green on the compass), and as the window nears, the captain orders it as a course order (again if the bow wanders off). The ship warps once the drive is full and the bow is on it (±10°); if the drive is full and you're still off it 20 s after the window opens, the captain takes the conn.
        13. Autopilot. T turns it on and holds the heading; tap the compass across the top to fly to that heading, or tap the captain's order to fly there. On autopilot the strip at the far right of the screen (clear of the battle) is the course set: drag it up and down (headings rise upward, blue box in the middle; the strip's whole height is 360°, so one sweep of the thumb reaches any heading) or tap a heading on it, and the autopilot speeds up and slows the turn to bring her onto it. For the last degree or two, the thin ▲ +1° and ▼ −1° buttons at the ends of the strip (or ] and [) nudge the course one degree; by hand they put her on autopilot one degree off the heading she's on. The compass across the top always shows the ship's actual heading. By hand (autopilot off) the strip is a wheel that slides up and down instead, with no numbers: the farther you slide it from the middle, the harder she turns (down for starboard, up for port), and it stays where you leave it; bringing her onto the heading is up to you. It flies carefully (never past the safe rate, even with a damaged engine; never with split engines) and earns less reputation than steering by hand.

        On the bridge

        The captain plots the course and gives the orders; his crew carry them out. For now the bridge is a test: title screen, Tests, Captain bridge, with the ship picked beside it (gunboat, picket ship, destroyer escort, destroyer, light cruiser, cruiser, heavy cruiser, battleship or dreadnought, and the gunboat and destroyer escort on escort missions), with no career effect. On a gunboat you're the coxswain: the pilot launches from the carrier's tube at once, and each of the three sorties ends at the carrier's recovery zone instead of a warp: be in it, on its heading, once the sortie timer runs out, and order RECOVER (J). A gunboat has no warp drive, so the engineer has no DRIVE stance. On a gunboat the pilot leaves the tube at the course's speed, not flat out.

        Escort missions (pick "Gunboat (escort mission)" or "Destroyer escort (escort mission)" beside the bridge test): your ship is one of a squadron of 2 to 4 screening a ward. A gunboat squadron screens the carrier each sortie, and she recovers your gunboat only from ASTERN of her: take that station (4) before the recovery, and the pilot holds it there till she takes you aboard; a destroyer escort squadron screens either a convoy of 3 to 5 merchants or a capital ship (rolled for each mission) through every system. She sails her own course to the recovery or warp zone (the blue ships on the map and in the battle view, each with a hull bar); there's no plotting screen. The pilot keeps your station on her (the dashed ring on the map: green when you're on it): AHEAD, PORT, STBD or ASTERN (1 to 4); a course or engine order of your own takes you off it until RESUME (R). Your wingmen (small teal ships) hold other stations; a teal station button has a wingman on it, and picking it swaps you: he flies over to your old station, and any enemy group after him follows him there. She sails her course like a ship, curving round each corner, and her convoy or stations swing round with her. A wingman guards his side of her and shoots down about half the torpedoes coming at her from it; now and then one hits him, and a wingman whose hull runs out is lost and his station opens. Each enemy group picks its target as it arrives and forms up round it: half of them go after her, the rest after a ship of the squadron (you or a wingman, alike), and a group fires only at its own target, so you take only your share. A group whose wingman is lost turns on her. Your gun crew shoots down what they can reach at her and the wingmen, so stay close. When a burst is coming at her, the TORPEDOES box says so: COVER (Q) puts your ship in its way and your shields take it instead, but only when you're close to the ship it's aimed at ("Too far to cover" otherwise). Once she reaches the zone the pilot brings you in on its heading; order RECOVER or WARP as usual. A carrier or capital ship whose hull runs out is lost, and so is the mission; a carrier down to 20% has lost her hangar bay and warps without you, and the mission fails (left behind); a convoy is lost only when every merchant is. Score: the objective is her hull at the end (a convoy: the share of its merchants that get through); navigation is the share of each fight you spent on station.

        Picket ship: scouting. A picket scouts: each system has 2 to 4 checkpoints (numbered cyan rings on the map) to fly through, in order, before the warp zone. The navigator's plot goes through all of them, and you get a leg more for each one. A plot that misses one shows "Misses checkpoint" and may still be accepted. A checkpoint turns green with a ✓ once you've flown through it, and the NAVIGATION box counts them (CP 1/3). Navigation score on a scouting run: half for reaching the zone on time, half for the share of checkpoints flown through.

        1. Plot the course. In warp space before each system, with the clock stopped, the plotting screen shows the system's map: where you come out of warp space, the warp zone (green), asteroid fields (grey) and enemy areas (red), in all shapes, with one always across the straight way to the warp. Each system's warp heading is set for you: the green arrow through the zone, with a dashed approach line leading in. Tap the map to lay waypoints: 1 to 8 legs to the warp zone, the last one along the warp heading (within 10°; Accept stays off until it is). A new plot starts on the approach line, and waypoints you lay go in before it. Tap a waypoint to remove it, and a leg's engine order (cruise, standard, full) to change it. The time line shows when the course reaches the warp zone against the warp window (the navigator aims a little before it). A leg through an enemy area shows red, and Accept stays off until the course goes round them. A leg through an asteroid field shows amber ("Through asteroids"): you may take it, but there are rocks to dodge and shoot. Navigator's plot fills in a safe course to accept or edit. The last leg's heading is the warp heading.
        2. The pilot flies it. He steers for the plotted legs and gives the engines each leg's order. NAVIGATION on the BRIDGE panel shows the leg, the next course change and the warp window; the map in the middle shows the ship on the plot. In the warp zone he holds her there, slow and then stopped, for the warp.
        3. The map. Its top line, ZONE ARRIVAL IN, counts down to the warp zone at her speed now, along the rest of the plot: faster and it drops, slower and it climbs. The next, CAN WARP IN, counts down to when the warp can go: the window open and the drive full, at the rate the drive is charging now (whichever is later: WINDOW or DRIVE; or DRIVE NOT CHARGING). On a dreadnought a third line, MOAB FULL IN, counts down to the planet buster full. Against the second line the top line says EARLY (slow down, or she waits in the zone), ON TIME or LATE (more speed). Drag it to pan, pinch or use + − to zoom, FIT for the whole map. The map starts in the middle with the battle view in the box at the upper right; ⇄ SWAP (V) trades them, and again to go back.
        4. A course of your own. ← → (or ◀ 10° and 10° ▶), a tap on the compass, or a tap on the map gives the helm a course of your own, and the engine buttons an engine order. HELM shows "yours" until you tap RESUME (R); then the pilot comes back onto the plotted line, never cutting across a field.
        5. Torpedoes. The pilot breaks on his own, by your standing policy for him (B): EVADE (he breaks for every burst he can: fewer hits, but she wanders off the plot and arrives later), HOLD (the standing order: he breaks only when the shields can't take it) or STEADY (no breaks: for a minefield, a main-gun swing or the last run into the zone). He breaks at the last moment, away from asteroid fields. For any one burst you can override him: ◀ BREAK (to port), TAKE IT or BREAK ▶ (to starboard) (Z X C) before they land. A break made in time makes them miss. A light cruiser and anything bigger turns too slowly to dodge a torpedo, so it has no break buttons or pilot policy: its shields take every burst. Break away from asteroid fields: the map shows where they are.
        6. The crew. Your crew act on your orders; they don't cover for you. The gunners' order (G) goes to every gun: FREE (the standing order: each gunner picks his own, torpedoes about to land, else the nearest ship), TORP (point defense: torpedoes about to land first), LAUNCH (ships charging to fire first), or HUNT (the ships each gun is best against). Nobody picks the order for the fight but you; the GUN meter over them is all the guns' charge together. Over the ENGINEER buttons, RX is the reactors' output, 0 to 150% of their rating (the tick is 100%; orange when one is overloaded), then the shields (SH), the drive (DR) and the other charges; the red bar under them is the reactors' heat. The engineer's stance (S) is his priority order: balanced, shields first, guns first, or drive first. Only on SHIELDS does he charge the shields ahead of the torpedoes he sees coming (and bridge a gap from the aux bank); on any other stance he just keeps them topping up and reacts only when a burst is a couple of seconds out, too late to fill them. But SHIELDS costs the guns: on it they get only a small share of their power, and the ships that launch the torpedoes keep coming. Go to SHIELDS for the torpedoes and back to BALANCE or GUNS to shoot. Normally the drive gets only the power left over after the shields and guns; on DRIVE it comes first after life support, charged fast enough to be full just before the window (flat out once the window is near or open, and over its last percent), and the shields and guns share what's left. Use it when the READY line on the map says DRIVE, and go back when it says WINDOW: the shields run lower on DRIVE. On a ship with a main gun, MAIN GUN puts it first after life support and charges it flat out, fight or no fight, so it's ready sooner for your MAIN GUN order; the shields and other guns share what's left. On a dreadnought, MOAB does the same for the planet buster (PB under ENGINEER), which must be full for the final warp; the MOAB bar under WARP and the map's top-left corner show when it'll be full at the rate it's charging. Warn engineering (E) before a hard burn.
        7. Scouting reports. The enemy named for each system (the briefing, the plotting screen, Tactical's "expect…" on arrival) is the pickets' survey, and they miss now and then: about 1 time in 5 something else entirely shows up. Watch what Tactical calls in and change your orders to match. Minefields are always reported right.
        8. Enemy areas. The red areas on the map are enemy country. Your plot can't cross one, but a break or a course order can carry you into one: then a swarm comes out of it, several times a normal fight. Fly out again and half of what's left of the swarm goes back; the other half stays on you. Keep breaks and turns clear of them.
        9. Orders take time. A new stance takes a few seconds to reach the engineer, and a new gun order a few seconds to reach each gun crew; the less skilled the crewman, the longer (skill 50 takes twice as long as skill 100). A new break policy (B) likewise takes a couple of seconds to reach the pilot, by his skill. Until an order is carried out, its button blinks and a bar fills along its bottom; when the bar is full, the crew are doing it. Give the order before you need it.
        10. Asteroids and minefields. You may fly into an asteroid field. Its rocks sit in the field where the map shows it, so the battle view shows them only when she's in it or close by; they come at her faster the faster she goes (none while she's stopped): slow down to make it safer. A mine scenario (the minefield, the gunboat's mine lane) has one big minefield (amber, MINEFIELD) over most of the way to the warp: a course always crosses it ("Through the minefield"), and its mines work the same way. Slowing down there (cruise or less) makes a big difference. The pilot steers round the ones coming at her and the gunners shoot them; one that touches the shield hits like a mine (90 MJ, shields first). The rocks and mines hit the enemy ships around her too: an enemy ship one touches is damaged, and small ones can be wrecked. A plotted leg that's clear keeps her out of them.
        11. The warp. Order it (J or WARP) once the drive is full, the window is open, the ship is in the zone and she's on the warp heading (within 5°); the WARP button says what's missing ("Steer 245"). Keeping her on the warp heading is your job: on the plot the pilot flies the last leg along it, and he holds her in the zone; on a course of your own, steer it yourself or RESUME PLOT. An ordered warp waits until she's in the zone (BACK TO THE ZONE) and on the heading (STEER 245), then goes.
        12. The drop (dreadnought, last system). No warp out: the ship drops the planet buster (MOAB) on the Azurian target world from the DROP ZONE. No heading and no drive are needed: get her into the zone, with the MOAB full (MOAB under ENGINEER puts it first), and order DROP MOAB (J); she may arrive early and wait, but must stay in the zone, and it goes when the window opens. The map's corner says CAN DROP IN, then DROP WINDOW CLOSES IN: if the planet buster isn't away 10 s after the window opens, the mission fails. Watch ZONE ARRIVAL: LATE here is a failure, not just a lower score.
        13. Main gun and gunboats (cruisers and up). With the main gun charged, MAIN GUN (M) has the pilot swing the bow onto the biggest ship out there (the next biggest if it goes first) and hold it there until the crew fires, then come back to the plot; HELM shows "main gun" meanwhile. The crew never fires the main gun without your order. A swing takes her off the plot (watch EARLY and LATE on the map), and a slow ship bow-on to an enemy has fewer guns on the torpedoes coming from ahead. On a cruiser and up, launching and recovering the gunboat squadron is yours alone: nobody else does it. The engineer keeps the hangar charging, the launchers while the squadron is aboard (LN under ENGINEER) and the arresters while it's out (AR); once the meter is full, LAUNCH / RECOVER (L) goes at once (until then the button shows the charge). The ship won't warp with the squadron out (WARP says "Recover gunboats"), unless her hull is nearly gone. In a minefield the squadron flies ahead of the bow and shoots the mines coming at her: launch it. GUNS on the BRIDGE panel shows the main gun's charge (or SWINGING) and where the gunboats are.
        14. Score. Objective and ship condition (the captain answers for his ship: every bit of hull lost costs twice what it does at the other stations), navigation (in the warp zone by the window, no asteroid hits) and command (the shields and guns against the torpedoes, and the breaks made, yours and the pilot's).

        Pilot training

        From the career screen, Pilot training puts you at the helm of any ship you're qualified on as a pilot (the gunboat if you aren't yet) on an endless buoy course: no enemies, no captain's orders, all the usual controls. Keep red buoys to port (on your left) and green to starboard; the next one is ringed, with a chevron on the side to pass, and marked in its color on the compass and the ring. The helm panel shows its side, bearing and seconds away, and a tally: passed on the right side, wrong side, hit (the hull touched it) and missed (too wide, or you wandered off and the course starts again ahead). The rating is the share passed on the right side: Excellent 90% and up, Good 75%, Fair 50%, else Needs work. Turning too hard still fills the stress bar and counts structural hits, but does the hull no harm. Fly as long as you like; Exit training shows the summary. Training earns no reputation and isn't saved to your record.
